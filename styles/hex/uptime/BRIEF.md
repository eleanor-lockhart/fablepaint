# UPTIME: the storyboard

The major piece in the Hex style. Seventy-two seconds, 1280 x 720, 24 frames a second, with a score.

**One sentence.** In the last minute of 1999 everything outside a small server waits for it to fail at midnight,
and the witch who keeps it declines.

**The feeling it is after** is the turn of the millennium: an operating system that was sure it was the future,
a network small enough for one person to keep, and a date everybody had been told to be afraid of.

**The grid.** 6/8 at one bar a second. An eighth note is four frames. The clock in the corner shows real time,
from 23:59:10 to 00:00:22, and it ticks on every downbeat: the system clock is the metronome. Every event below
sits on a bar line.

**The stage.** A desktop. Wallpaper of hexagonal cells in the night registers. A column of icons. Three windows:
TOWER (the eight-bit scene from stage 1, live, at twice its size; its title bar is green because it is hers),
WEB RING (five small homepages, each with one lit window in register F and one inhabitant, and a sixth under
construction), and PROCESSES (WITCH.EXE, PID 1, status ALWAYS). A taskbar with the clock.

**Whose is what.** System dialogs have blue bars. The hive's are magenta and so is its pointer. Hers are green
and so is her pointer. Register F is the tower window and the five homepages and nothing else, and only F glows.

**What it must say to a stranger** (for the cold reading):

1. Somebody is in the lit window, and she is in charge of this machine.
2. A crowd wants in and she turns it into newts.
3. Something takes over the screen and tries to shut her down, and cannot.
4. Everybody waits for midnight expecting the machine to fail.
5. At midnight the small lights go out and hers does not.
6. She orders the date to be right and it is; the lights come back.
7. She is not nice. The place is safe anyway.
