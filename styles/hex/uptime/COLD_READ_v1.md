# UPTIME, first cut: a cold reading

A fresh agent that had been told nothing was given 36 contact sheets (three frames for every second of the
film, each captioned with its time) and asked to say what happens. It could not hear the score and it saw
frames at half size. Its closing sections are given here as it wrote them, and its narration is summarised in
the scoring table with its own words in quotation marks. The second cut was made from this.

## Scored against the storyboard

| what the film must say | landed? | the reader's words |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Somebody is in the lit window, and she is in charge of this machine | yes | "The witch (WITCH.EXE, PID 1, 'ALWAYS'). She is never shown as a figure. She exists as the lit tower window, the green text, and the green pointer that rests at the tower door." |
| 2. A crowd wants in and she turns it into newts | yes | "It never touches ALLOW or DENY." It also worked out that 111435 "happens to be the sum of all eight requests". |
| 3. Something takes over the screen and tries to shut her down, and cannot | yes | "I take this to be the crowd's or public's verdict on what the witch just did, taking the machine over." "She answers without lifting a finger." |
| 4. Everybody waits for midnight expecting the machine to fail | half | "I can't tell what they want; it might be to watch the end." "I don't know who launched THE_END.EXE." |
| 5. At midnight the small lights go out and hers does not | yes | "The end that was promised turned into a slow breakdown instead of a stop." |
| 6. She orders the date to be right and it is; the lights come back | yes | "She issues her own decree and confirms it herself." |
| 7. She is not nice. The place is safe anyway | half | "She deals with intruders and objections by turning them into newts." Safety it got; that she is malevolent it did not say. |

Five of seven, and two halves. My own prediction, sealed in the journal beforehand, was four and three halves:
I expected the reader to miss that the green pointer is hers, and it did not.

## Faults it found

- **The hive came back.** "Oddly, at 56.00 the pink background is back over the whole desktop." At midnight I
  turned the hive grey, and the reader took grey for gone; at the decree it turned pink again before being
  cleared. A state that has ended was shown again.
- **Fourteen flat seconds.** "For these fourteen seconds nothing else changes. The crowd stands still and the
  witch's pointer stays at her door. It is a long, flat stretch." And: "the countdown in particular felt like
  waiting rather than tension."
- **A slow opening.** "For about seven seconds almost nothing happens." My gag there, the witch straightening
  an icon that is out of line, read as "the green pointer wanders". The icon was nine pixels out.
- **Who the watchers are.** "web ring residents? the newted users back again? spectators come to watch the end?"
- **The hive's icon stayed pink** after the process list said it was a newt, and the pointer turning into a
  newt read as "a single small green newt crawls along the edge of the countdown window".
- **Too small to read:** the counters on the homepages and the number on the bin.

## The reader's own closing sections

**The story in three sentences.** "At 23:59 on 31 December 1999, an ancient witch who has always been running in
her tower answers a flood of connection requests by turning all of them into newts. A pink consensus spreads
across the machine and tries to shut her down, she refuses ('SHE IS.'), and a countdown to 'everything stops'
runs to midnight, where the clock breaks to 1900 instead and the web ring goes dark. She declares 'IT IS THE
YEAR 2000 BECAUSE I SAY SO,' and with that the clock is fixed, the consensus becomes a newt, the neighbours
come back, and her one window stays lit while everything else fades."

**The three moments it understood least.** The crowd along the bottom from 35 to 61 seconds and the
"orange-topped blocks on the hill"; the pink returning at 56 seconds, the icon that stayed pink, and who
launched THE_END.EXE; and "the small details that look like they mean something but I can't decode: 'REGISTER
F ... RESERVED', the colour strip at the start and end, the hit counters reading 437, and the drone flying
into the spider's tile near the end."

**The three moments that worked best, and whether it was funny.** "'TASK DECLINES.' / 'ARE YOU SURE?' / 'SHE
IS.' This is the best joke in the film, and it lands harder because the witch's pointer never moves." Then
"the doubling stack of GATE.EXE dialogs, ending in one click on NEWTS (a button sitting between ALLOW and DENY
as if it were a normal choice)". Then the decree "together with the ending ... The last image says the whole
idea in one picture." And: "Was it funny: yes, in places. 'SHE IS.' made me laugh."

On the drone that flies into the spider's page before its counter goes up by one: "Either someone visited the
spider's site through the ring, or the spider ate a drone. I think both readings are meant." They are now.
