# Base Case, second cut: a second cold reading, of the second half only

A new reader (a reader works once) was given the last 48 seconds of the second cut, from the moment the picture
enters the 1980 machine, on 24 sheets at six frames a second. It was told that this was the second half of a
film and nothing else. The first half was not re-tested: its beats had landed. Times below are counted from the
start of the half; add 48 for the film's time.

No questions were sent afterwards. The narration had already answered each of them (her hat, who presses
RETURN, what F is, what is on the wall), and a resumed reader costs as much as a new one.

## The narration, unedited

I read all 24 sheets in order (frames 0 to 287, 0.00 to 47.83 s) and looked at nothing else.

THE NARRATION

**Something on screen the whole time (until 44.00 s).** A small box sits in the top right corner. Its title bar says "STACK" and it holds a numbered list. Under the list is a strip of about 16 coloured squares with tiny numbers. At 0.00 the list already reads "0 DESCEND(1998) / 1 DESCEND(1989) / 2 DESCEND(1987)", and the last line is brighter than the others. Lines are added and taken off as the film goes on. It looks like a programmer's call stack: things that have been called and haven't finished yet.

**0.00–0.17.** The screen is black. A thin white horizontal line splits it into a picture area on top and a text area below.

**0.33.** A white line drawing appears on black: a floor in perspective with a rectangle marked on it. Text: "PANTRY. THERE IS A TRAPDOOR HERE." The look is an old computer text adventure with simple line pictures.

**1.17.** Slanted shelves with upright rectangular items are drawn in on the left and right, which fits a pantry. Some small marks show at the top edge near the stack box, probably the tops of things on a shelf.

**1.33–2.33.** A ">" prompt appears. Someone I never see types "LIFT TRAPDOOR" one letter at a time. At 2.33 the reply is "OK.", and the rectangle in the floor turns into an upright panel with a small handle-like stroke. I take it the trapdoor is now open.

**2.83–3.00.** "DOWN" is typed. At 3.00 the picture goes completely black and a new line appears in the stack: "3 DESCEND(1980)".

**3.17–5.83.** A new line drawing builds up piece by piece: ceiling beams, posts, stairs on the left, tall box shapes (could be appliances or cabinets), a small set of drawers, and a desk on the right with a box shape behind it. Text at 3.33: "BASEMENT. THERE IS NO COMPUTER HERE." The sentence is odd. It reads as if the player expected a computer to be there, as if basements usually have one.

**6.00.** A coloured figure appears at the desk. Everything else is still white lines. She has a green face, a long nose pointing left, a pointed purple hat with a pink band, and a dark violet robe. Text: "THERE IS A WITCH HERE." The stack gains "4 WITCH() BASE CASE" in green, and its title bar turns from blue to teal-green. She stays still the whole time she is on screen. Her face seems turned to the left, though at this size I'm not sure.

**8.17–10.00.** "ASK WITCH FOR LIGHT" is typed. The reply is "SHE DOES NOT TAKE REQUESTS." The text has scrolled, so the PANTRY line is now gone.

**11.83–13.00.** "SAY PLEASE" is typed. The reply is "I DON'T KNOW THE WORD "PLEASE"."

**14.50–16.00.** "LOOK AT WITCH" is typed. The reply is "SHE WAS ALREADY LOOKING AT YOU." The player gets nowhere. The tone is a little menacing.

**16.67–17.83.** An empty prompt with a blinking cursor. The player types nothing.

**18.00.** A grey dialog box appears on the left of the picture. Its title bar says "WITCH", and it has a small close box. It reads "IT IS DARK IN MY HOUSE. I DID NOT ALLOW THAT." Below is a green rounded button labelled "RETURN". I take this as the witch speaking for herself, not answering the player. She calls it her house, and she objects to the dark.

**18.67–21.83.** A small arrow-shaped mouse pointer appears near the witch's hands and the drawers at her desk. It wanders around her for a moment, slides under the dialog, moves left along its bottom edge, and from about 21.00 rests on RETURN. Because it starts at her and the next line says "she presses", I take it that the witch is moving the pointer. The player's prompt sits empty throughout.

**22.00.** The whole frame flashes a warm yellow-gold. New text in yellow: "SHE PRESSES RETURN. THE VALUE IS F." From here on the basement lines are drawn in gold instead of white, which I read as the basement now being lit. The dialog is gone by 22.50.

**22.83.** The "4 WITCH() BASE CASE" line leaves the stack and the title bar goes back to blue. The witch is still drawn at the desk.

**23.00.** Yellow text: "F IS A LIT WINDOW." So whatever F is, the film defines it as a lit window. (In programming F could also mean "false". The film never says so; that's only my second reading.)

**24.00.** Another gold flash, and the stack line becomes "3 DESCEND(1980) = F". From 24.17 the camera pulls back. The whole text-adventure screen shrinks inside a white frame with grey dotted shading.

**24.83–25.83.** The "3 DESCEND(1980)" line leaves the stack. The pull-back shows the screen sitting inside a black-and-white window titled "The House", with a striped title bar like an old desktop computer. At 25.50 a menu bar appears above it: a small glyph, then "File Edit Go Cards". The sides of the window are filled with grey dot patterns, including what looks like brickwork. A line below the inner screen reads "YOU KNOW HOW THIS GOES." I take it as someone (the film, or a narrator) telling the viewer that the same step will now repeat.

**26.00.** A gold flash, and the line becomes "2 DESCEND(1987) = F". At 26.83 it leaves the stack. The camera keeps pulling back.

**27.17.** A new bar of text appears at the top of the frame: "BASE CASE   LIGHTS: ? OF 5". The character after "LIGHTS:" could be a 7 or a question mark; at this size I can't tell which. There are small pink marks under some of the letters. This outer layer has pale lavender-blue bricks at the right edge. The "File Edit Go Cards" window now sits inside it.

**28.00.** A gold flash, and the line becomes "1 DESCEND(1989) = F". At 28.83 it leaves the stack, leaving only "0 DESCEND(1998)". The nested pictures inside get coarser and noisier, and the inner text turns into unreadable scribbles.

**29.50–29.83.** The flat picture tilts. It turns out to be the screen of a cream-coloured box monitor standing in a solid 3D room built from blocks: grey brick walls, dark ceiling beams.

**30.00–31.83.** The camera swings back and around. A large rounded purple shape at the left edge turns out to be the back of someone's head. By 31.00 we see her from behind: purple hair, a blue top, a dark brown chair, a wooden desk with a pale grey slab that is probably a keyboard, and the cream monitor. Above the monitor is a shelf of small boxes in cyan, pink and navy. The floor is speckled grey and there is a white box at the far right. She doesn't move.

**32.00.** A gold flash, and the line becomes "0 DESCEND(1998) = F". The picture cuts to a view facing her. She is a small girl figure with purple hair, a pale grey-blue face, two dark eyes and a blue top. A glowing cyan bar sits at her lap below the desk edge; I can't tell what it is. The back of the monitor is a dark grey box beside her. On the brick wall to her left is a large dark shadow, much bigger than she is: a pointed hat, a long hooked nose, a jutting chin and a robe-like body. It is a witch's silhouette, facing her. I see no shadow shaped like the girl herself.

**32.67.** As the camera drifts slightly, a white column with an orange band comes into view at the left edge. Judging by the later outside shot, it is the white-and-orange cylinder in the basement.

**32.83.** The stack now reads "(EMPTY)".

**34.00.** A small green rectangle starts to glow where the shadow's eye would be. The shadow cannot be an ordinary shadow, since it has a glowing eye.

**35.00–35.67.** The lighting flickers. The shadow and the monitor back go lighter and darker in alternate frames.

**36.00–39.83.** The shadow settles to a lighter grey but stays on the wall with its green eye. The monitor back stays a lighter grey-brown. The girl never moves and never looks at the shadow. The camera drifts slowly.

**40.00.** A cut to outside at night. The view is a cross-section: a long, low wooden house with dark windows, and below the ground a cut-away basement, lit and visible. From left to right it holds shelves, brown stairs, red poles, a tall teal box, a white cylinder with an orange band (perhaps a water heater), a thin yellow strip of light at the ceiling, and the desk with the cream monitor and a small blue mark. The blue mark may be the girl, but she is too small to make out. At this size I can't see the shadow on the wall. The ground is dark navy and speckled, and white flakes are falling, so it is snowing.

**40.17–41.83.** The camera rises and pulls back. It shows the house's brown plank walls and four-pane windows, then a grey snowy roof, a grey door at the right, a railing at the right, a chimney with white smoke puffs, dark pine trees behind, and a night sky full of snow. A small reddish X-shaped mark sits on the dark ground at the right; it could be a bare bush.

**42.00–43.50.** The house's windows light up warm yellow one at a time, from left to right. There is one at 42.00, then 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (the window left of the door), and by 43.50 all seven, including the one right of the door. I take this as the returned value, "F is a lit window", reaching the real house. The light starts in the basement and spreads upward.

**44.00.** The stack box disappears from the corner for good.

**44.50.** Large white blocky letters over the snowy ground read "BASE CASE".

**45.00.** A smaller line underneath reads "EVERY HOUSE HAS ONE."

**45.00–47.83.** Everything holds: snow falling, smoke rising, seven lit windows, the basement lit below, the title on screen. The film ends at 47.83.

WHO OR WHAT IS IN IT, AS FAR AS I CAN TELL

- **The unseen typist.** Someone types the commands at the ">" prompt (lift trapdoor, down, ask, say please, look). I never see who, and they type nothing after 16.67 s. My guess is the girl at the computer, since we later see that every screen we watched sits inside her monitor. I never actually see her type.
- **The text voice.** It answers the commands ("OK.", "I DON'T KNOW THE WORD...", "SHE WAS ALREADY LOOKING AT YOU."). A separate line, "YOU KNOW HOW THIS GOES.", appears in the "The House" window. That one might be the same voice or the film itself.
- **The witch.** She has a green face, a purple pointed hat with a pink band and a dark violet robe, and sits at a desk at the bottom of the trapdoor. She is the only coloured thing in the line-drawn basement. She calls it "my house", refuses requests, ignores "please" and doesn't allow darkness. She works the mouse pointer and presses RETURN herself. The stack labels her "WITCH() BASE CASE".
- **The girl.** She has purple hair, a pale grey-blue face and a blue top, and sits at a cream computer in a brick-walled basement with stairs, poles and a white-and-orange cylinder. She is the outermost person we see. She doesn't move.
- **The witch-shaped shadow.** It is on the wall beside the girl, larger than her, and from 34.00 has a glowing green eye. I can't say whose it is.
- **The house.** It is long, low and wooden, with a snowy roof, a smoking chimney and a basement, standing among pines at night in falling snow. By the end its seven windows are lit.
- **The stack box.** It lists nested steps: DESCEND with the years 1998, 1989, 1987 and 1980, then WITCH. They are taken off one by one, each marked "= F", until the box says "(EMPTY)" and then vanishes. The years fit the look of each nested layer if each layer is a computer from that year: blocky 3D room (1998), dotted game screen titled "BASE CASE" (1989), black-and-white windowed desktop (1987), white line-and-text game (1980). That is my inference, not something the film says.

THE STORY OF THIS HALF, IN THREE SENTENCES

In an old line-drawn text game, someone opens a pantry trapdoor and goes down into a basement that, the game notes, has no computer, only a witch, who refuses to be asked for light, doesn't know "please" and was already watching. Then, unasked and saying it is dark in her house and she did not allow that, the witch clicks RETURN herself, and the answer "F", "a lit window", is passed back up through screen after screen nested inside one another, each layer flashing gold as it finishes, until we reach a purple-haired girl at a computer in a real-looking basement with a witch's shadow on the wall beside her. Outside in the snow the house's windows light up one by one, and the title reads "BASE CASE — EVERY HOUSE HAS ONE."

THE THREE MOMENTS I UNDERSTOOD LEAST

1. **The witch shadow next to the girl (32.00–39.83, green eye from 34.00).** I can't tell whose shadow it is or what it means. It might be the witch who has come up with the returned value, the girl's own shadow showing her as a witch, something standing off-screen, or a protector rather than a threat. The girl never reacts, and the shadow fades a little around 35 s without leaving.
2. **The descent at 3.00.** Typing DOWN adds "DESCEND(1980)" to the stack, yet the pantry before and the basement after are drawn in exactly the same style and share one continuous block of text. So I can't square what the years mean with where the layer boundaries are. I also don't know why "THERE IS NO COMPUTER HERE" is the first thing said about the basement, though I suspect it means this is the bottom layer: no computer means nowhere further down to go.
3. **"BASE CASE LIGHTS: ? OF 5" (27.17–about 30).** I couldn't read whether the count is a 7 or a question mark. "Of 5" might be the five stack entries (four DESCENDs plus WITCH), but the house ends with seven lit windows, not five, and I can't make the numbers agree.

Beat 8 is half, and I am leaving it there. The shadow is now seen for what it is, and the reader noticed, unasked,
that the child has no other shadow. That the film does not say which of the four readings is right is the
piece's choice and not its fault: nothing winks and nothing is explained.

Still not right, from this reading:

- LIGHTS: 7 OF 5 cannot be read at the size it has on the way out, and this reader had not seen the five

- DESCEND(1980) is pushed when the stairs are taken, three seconds after the 1980 picture begins, so the stack
