# Base Case, first cut: the cold reading

A fresh agent (no repository instructions, read-only) was given 48 contact sheets holding the film at six frames
a second, two seconds to a sheet, and nothing else. Its narration is below, unedited; then its answers to
questions it was sent only afterwards; then the score against the eight beats in `BRIEF.md`, which it never saw.
My own verdict was written and sealed first (journal, entry 6).

## The narration, unedited

I read all 48 sheets in order: frames 0–575, 0.00 s to 95.83 s, six frames a second. It looks like a video-game render. The camera is smooth and the figures are made of blocks. A small box sits in the top-right corner the whole time. Its blue title bar says "STACK", and below that it starts with "(EMPTY)". Its contents change during the film, and I note each change.

**Narration**

0.00–4.83 s. Night with falling snow and dark pine trees behind. A long, low, one-storey house is seen side-on. It has a grey speckled roof, brown plank walls, a small chimney, a railing on the right part of the roof, a grey door near the right end and grey snow banks at each side. Of its 7 windows, 5 glow warm yellow and 2 are dark (the 3rd, and the one just left of the door). Under the house the ground is cut away like a cross-section, showing a dim basement. A small pale patch with some orange sits toward its right. The camera slowly pushes in.

5.00 s. All the windows go dark at once. Nothing on screen shows why. The camera keeps pushing in toward the house and the cut-away basement until 7.83 s.

8.00 s. Cut to a 3D interior. A narrow passage runs between tall dark shelves holding small pink, teal and dark boxes or jars. A small blocky figure is seen from behind. It has a large rounded dark-purple head of hair, a blue top and trousers, a teal-cyan band at the waist and grey shoes. At the far end, bright white light comes through what looks like a window onto an orange-lit floor. A rectangle on that floor is outlined in glowing yellow. A long stick with an orange-brown end leans against the left shelves; I take it to be a broom. The figure walks toward the rectangle (8–11 s) as the camera rises to look down on it.

11.00–12.67 s. The figure stands inside the yellow rectangle. A dark brown flat panel rises upright in front of it, and a grey speckled opening appears at its feet. I read this as a trapdoor being lifted. I did not clearly see the figure bend to lift it.

12.83–15.83 s. The camera drops lower. At 13.00 the STACK box changes to "0 DESCEND(1998)". The figure sinks lower into the opening, legs then body disappearing. I take it to be going down stairs.

16.00 s. Cut to a side view of a basement. It has dark brick walls, a wooden-beam ceiling, a diagonal staircase and thin maroon posts. There is also a tall dark green-black box with a small light square, a column of orange bricks (perhaps a chimney), a tall white cylinder with an orange band (perhaps a water heater) and small boxes under the stairs. At 16.50 a small blue shape appears at the top of the stairs. The figure walks down from 16.5 to 20.5 s; now in profile, it has a pale grey face. The camera pans right, revealing a lit corner with pale brick, a desk, a chair, a beige computer monitor, a small box and shelves of coloured items.

21.50–22.17 s. The white cylinder briefly flares bright white with orange at its base, then returns to normal by 22.33. Around the same time a small grey shape with lighter spots sits on the floor near the figure's feet and stays there as the camera moves. I cannot tell what either is. The flare might be a flame or a lighting effect; the grey shape might be a mouse, a rag or a dropped toy.

22.5–25.83 s. The figure walks to the desk and sits facing the monitor, its back to us.

26.00–31.83 s. Over-the-shoulder shot of the purple hair, a dark chair back, a wooden desk, a keyboard and a beige boxy CRT monitor. The camera pushes into the screen until it fills the frame. On it, in blue-white dithered pixels, is the same house with all its windows dark. Across the top it reads "BASE CASE   LIGHTS: 0 OF 1". A white box reads "SO YOU WANT THE LIGHTS ON / PRESS ANY KEY".

32.83 s. The picture changes, which I take to mean a key was pressed. It shows the same shelf passage in a paler dithered blue palette, with the same child seen from behind and the broom, now with a bright orange head. A command line at the bottom fills letter by letter: ">LIFT TRAPDOOR" (32.83–33.83). We never see the child's hands typing.

34.00–35.83 s. A text box reads "IT IS HEAVY. YOU ARE NINE. IT OPENS ANYWAY." The child seems to crouch (34.5–34.83), stand, then sink down through the floor. At 35.33 the STACK box adds "1 DESCEND(1989)".

36.00–38.50 s. A side-view basement in the same dithered style, laid out like the earlier one: stairs, a dark box, a pink-red brick column and pink post, a cylinder with orange bands, a desk and a small beige computer box. The child walks down the stairs and right. At 37.33 a text box reads "A COMPUTER, LEFT ON. OF COURSE THERE IS." The child stops beside the small computer.

38.67–40.83 s. Zoom into a black-and-white, early-Macintosh-style screen. A menu bar reads "File Edit Go Cards", and a window is titled "The House". Inside is a dithered drawing looking down into the shelf passage, with a rectangle on the floor. The caption reads "A TRAP DOOR. YOU HAVE TO LIFT IT." A small arrow pointer moves onto the rectangle.

41.00–42.83 s. The picture flashes inverted (a click, I assume). The trapdoor is now open, with its lid up and speckled steps going down. The caption reads "STAIRS. THEY GO DOWN." The pointer moves onto the steps.

43.00–44.83 s. Another inverted flash, and the STACK box adds "2 DESCEND(1987)". A grainy dissolve leads to a black-and-white basement drawing with a tall white cylinder, shelves, a desk with a monitor and a white box. The caption reads "A BASEMENT. A COMPUTER, LEFT ON." The pointer goes to the monitor.

45.00–47.83 s. Inverted flash. A dark dithered circle grows from the monitor. The window then shows a big monitor frame around a dark screen, captioned "YOU KNOW HOW THIS GOES." A thin white horizontal line appears across the dark screen, and the camera pushes in until it fills the frame.

48.00–50.83 s. A black screen, cut by the white line, starts drawing itself in thin white lines. It shows a perspective passage with shelves on both sides. The text below reads "PANTRY. THERE IS A TRAPDOOR HERE." Then ">LIFT TRAPDOOR" is typed, the reply is "OK.", and the drawing shows the trapdoor open. Then ">DOWN" is typed.

51.00–53.83 s. The picture area clears, and the STACK box adds "3 DESCEND(1980)". White lines draw a basement piece by piece: tall vertical lines, shelves, a table, a box and a small slanted shape. The text reads "BASEMENT. THERE IS NO COMPUTER HERE." No computer is drawn.

54.00 s. A coloured pixel figure appears among the white lines, in front of the table. It has a square green face with at least one black eye, a wide flat purple hat with a pink-red end and a dark purple body or robe. The text adds "THERE IS A WITCH HERE." The STACK box adds "4 WITCH() BASE CASE" in green, and the box's title bar turns green. The witch does not move for the rest of this section.

56.00–65.83 s. Typed exchanges follow:
- ">ASK WITCH FOR LIGHT" gets "SHE DOES NOT TAKE REQUESTS."
- ">SAY PLEASE" gets "I DON'T KNOW THE WORD "PLEASE"."
- ">LOOK AT WITCH" gets "SHE WAS ALREADY LOOKING AT YOU."
Then the prompt sits empty with a blinking cursor.

66.00 s. A small window pops up at the upper left. Its style differs from the line-drawing game: a grey-blue body and a title bar labelled "WITCH". It reads "IT IS DARK IN MY HOUSE. / I DID NOT ALLOW THAT." and has a green button labelled "RETURN". From 67.00 a green arrow pointer appears near the table and drifts up and left onto RETURN, resting there by 69.33.

70.00 s. A yellow flash. The white line drawing turns yellow, and yellow text appears at the bottom: "RETURN F". The WITCH window is gone by 70.50. At 70.83 the "4 WITCH() BASE CASE" line disappears from the STACK box, and its title bar turns blue again.

72.00–79.83 s. The camera pulls back out through every layer. Each time, the image flashes yellow or sepia and the matching STACK line gets "= F" before it is removed:
- 72.00: "3 DESCEND(1980) = F", inside a monitor frame.
- 73.17–74.83: the Mac window ("YOU KNOW HOW THIS GOES." still showing), then "2 DESCEND(1987) = F".
- 75.33: the dithered layer's top line now reads "BASE CASE   LIGHTS: 1 OF 1", where before it read 0 OF 1.
- 76.00: "1 DESCEND(1989) = F".
- 77.5–79.83: back out of the beige 3D monitor to the child seated at the desk from behind. The shelf above holds cyan, pink and dark boxes, and the brick wall is pale and well lit.

80.00 s. Sepia flash, and "0 DESCEND(1998) = F". Cut to a front view of the child at the desk, seen past the dark back of the monitor. The child has a pale grey-lavender face with two dark dot eyes, no mouth I could see, purple hair framing the face and a blue top. A tall cylinder with an orange band stands in the left foreground with a thin orange pipe. On the brick wall behind the child is a large dark jagged shadow, a stepped triangle shaped like a pine tree. At 80.83 the STACK box returns to "(EMPTY)".

83.00–83.83 s. The light flickers, alternating darker and brighter frames. From 84.00 it stays bright: the cylinder is white-grey, the monitor's back is beige and the big shadow is a much paler grey. The child does not visibly move through 87.83, and the camera drifts slightly.

88.00–89.83 s. Cut outside to the cut-away basement, now clearly lit. The camera pulls up and back past the house's plank wall, whose windows are still dark. White blocky puffs are rising from the chimney; I take them to be smoke, and I did not see any at the start.

90.00–91.50 s. The windows light one at a time from left to right: one at 90.00, then two, three, four and five, six at 91.33 and seven at 91.50. All 7 are lit now, including the two that were dark at the start.

92.50 s. Large white pixel letters read "BASE CASE". At 93.00 smaller text below reads "EVERY HOUSE HAS ONE." The house stays lit with snow falling until 95.83 s, where the film ends.

**Who is in it**

- **The child.** Small and blocky, with big purple hair, blue clothes, a cyan waist band and a pale grey face. The text says "YOU ARE NINE", so I take the child to be nine. Its sex is never stated. The same child appears in the 1998 and 1989 layers; the 1987 and 1980 layers are seen through its eyes or only in text.
- **The witch.** Green face, flat purple hat, dark robe. She appears only in the bottom layer. The game text calls her "she" and the STACK box calls her "WITCH() BASE CASE". Her window says "my house". She might be the owner or spirit of the house, or stand for an adult in it, but this is a guess.
- **The house.** It appears in every layer, each time drawn more crudely: 3D (labelled 1998), dithered colour (1989), black-and-white Mac style (1987) and white line drawing (1980). Each layer has a pantry with a trapdoor and a basement. Each basement has a computer left on, except the last, which has the witch instead.
- **The STACK box.** It behaves like a running list of nested calls, with one line added per descent and removed on the way back.
- **Whoever types and clicks.** I assume it is the child, but I never see it happen.

**The story in three sentences**

On a snowy night the lights of a house go out, and a nine-year-old goes down through a pantry trapdoor to a basement, where a computer left on is running a game about the same house with its lights off. The child plays it and goes down that game's trapdoor into an older-looking basement and computer, and again and again, until the bottom basement has no computer, only a witch who will not take requests. She says "It is dark in my house. I did not allow that", her RETURN button is clicked, the answer passes back up through every layer, a light flickers on beside the child, and every window of the house lights up under the title "BASE CASE: EVERY HOUSE HAS ONE."

**The three moments I understood least**

1. **The witch's window and the green pointer (66–70 s).** I cannot tell whether "I did not allow that" means she objects to the dark and is fixing it, or something else. I also don't know who moves the green pointer that clicks RETURN: a line-drawing text game of that look would have no mouse, and the pointer is green like her window rather than black like the Mac one.
2. **"RETURN F" and "= F" (70–80 s).** Every layer passes back the value F. It might mean "false", a letter, a name or something else. "False" sits oddly with the lights then coming on.
3. **The big jagged shadow behind the child and the flicker (80–84 s).** I cannot tell what casts the large stepped-triangle shadow. It might be the child's own shadow stretched by a low light, or the shape of a pine tree. Nor do I know why the light flickers before staying on.

Smaller things I also could not explain:
- why the windows went out at 5.00 s;
- why 7 windows are lit at the end when only 5 were at the start;
- the cylinder's brief flare at 21.5 s;
- the small grey object on the basement floor at about 22 s.

## Its answers to the questions, unedited (sent only after the narration)

These answers come from memory of the sheets; I did not open them again.

**1. Where it takes place, and the lights at the start**
- It is a long, low, one-storey house at night, with snow falling and dark pine trees behind it. A cut-away view also shows the basement under the ground.
- Then come a passage lined with shelves inside the house, the basement itself, and the screens of computers in each basement.
- At the start 5 of the 7 windows are lit and 2 are dark: the 3rd window and the one just left of the door.
- At 5.00 s all the windows go dark at once. Nothing on screen shows what caused it.

**2. The small figure and what it does first**
- It is a small, blocky child with a large rounded head of dark purple hair and blue clothes. It has a cyan-teal band at the waist, grey shoes, and later a pale grey face with two dot eyes. Text inside the game says "YOU ARE NINE," so I take it to be nine years old.
- I saw nothing that says the child is a girl. The text says "you," and "she" is only ever used for the witch, so I wrote "it." The question's "she" is not something I saw.
- What it does in the first part:
  - From 8.00 s it walks down the shelf passage toward a light at the far end.
  - It stands inside a rectangle outlined in yellow on the floor. A dark brown panel rises upright and an opening appears, which I took to be a trapdoor being lifted. I did not clearly see the child lift it.
  - It sinks down through the opening.
  - It walks down the basement stairs (16.5–20.5 s) and across to a desk with a beige computer, and sits down (about 25.5 s).

**3. What is on the computer screen, and what happens when the picture goes in**
- The screen shows the same house in blue-white dithered pixels, with every window dark.
- The top line reads "BASE CASE   LIGHTS: 0 OF 1." A box reads "SO YOU WANT THE LIGHTS ON / PRESS ANY KEY."
- The camera pushes in until the screen fills the frame. At 32.83 s the picture becomes the same shelf passage in a paler dithered style, with the same child.
- The command ">LIFT TRAPDOOR" types itself out, and the reply is "IT IS HEAVY. YOU ARE NINE. IT OPENS ANYWAY."
- The child goes down into a basement in the same style. The text says "A COMPUTER, LEFT ON. OF COURSE THERE IS."
- The picture then goes into that computer as well, and the same pattern repeats.

**4. How many looks, and how they relate**
I counted four main looks.
- (a) Smooth, blocky 3D in full colour. This covers the snowy outside, the passage and the first basement. The STACK box gives the year as 1998.
- (b) Flat pixels with dithering in a limited palette of pale blues, with pink/red and orange spots. Year 1989.
- (c) Black and white, like an early Mac. It has a menu bar reading "File Edit Go Cards," a window titled "The House," and an arrow pointer that clicks. Year 1987.
- (d) Thin white lines on black, with typed commands and replies underneath. Year 1980.

There are also some extras:
- The witch is a coloured pixel figure inside the line-drawing look.
- Her pop-up window is in yet another style, with a grey-blue body and a green button.
- The return trip adds yellow and sepia flashes, and the yellow lines in look (d).

How they relate:
- Each look appears on a computer screen inside the one before it, so they are nested.
- Each looks older and cruder than the last, and the years count down.
- Each contains the same house: a shelf passage or pantry with a trapdoor, then a basement. Every basement has a computer except the 1980 one.
- At the end the camera passes back out through them in reverse order.

**5. The small window in the top right**
- It has a blue title bar reading "STACK." It starts as "(EMPTY)."
- It gains one line each time the child goes down:
  - "0 DESCEND(1998)" at 13.00 s
  - "1 DESCEND(1989)" at 35.33 s
  - "2 DESCEND(1987)" at 43.00 s
  - "3 DESCEND(1980)" at 51.00 s
  - "4 WITCH() BASE CASE" in green at 54.00 s, when the title bar also turns green.
- On the way back each line gets "= F" and is removed, until the window reads "(EMPTY)" again at 80.83 s.
- My guess is that it tracks how deep we are, like a list of nested steps where each one has to finish and hand back an answer. I cannot say what "F" means.

**6. The green figure: what is asked, how she answers**
- She has a square green face with at least one black eye, a wide flat purple hat with a pink-red end, and a dark purple body or robe.
- The text calls her a witch: "THERE IS A WITCH HERE." The STACK box calls her "WITCH() BASE CASE."
- She is in the 1980 basement, where the text says "THERE IS NO COMPUTER HERE."
- What was typed, and the replies:

| Typed | Reply |
|---|---|
| ">ASK WITCH FOR LIGHT" | "SHE DOES NOT TAKE REQUESTS." |
| ">SAY PLEASE" | "I DON'T KNOW THE WORD "PLEASE"." |
| ">LOOK AT WITCH" | "SHE WAS ALREADY LOOKING AT YOU." |

- I never saw who typed these.

**7. What the green figure does in the end, and why**
- I did not see the witch herself move or do anything. She stands still the whole time.
- At 66.00 s a small window titled "WITCH" appears. It reads "IT IS DARK IN MY HOUSE. / I DID NOT ALLOW THAT." and has a green "RETURN" button.
- A green arrow pointer moves onto RETURN. At 70.00 s there is a yellow flash and "RETURN F" appears in yellow.
- I did not see whose pointer it is, and I cannot be sure of her reason. Those are the only words that suggest one. My best reading is that she objects to the dark in "her" house, so she sends the light back, but that is a guess.

**8. What happens to each picture we came through**
On the way out each layer flashes yellow or sepia, its STACK line gets "= F," and the line is removed:
- 1980 line drawing: the lines turn yellow (70 s), then "3 … = F" at 72.00. It shrinks into a monitor frame.
- 1987 Mac window: still captioned "YOU KNOW HOW THIS GOES." "2 … = F" at 74.00.
- 1989 dithered layer: its top line now reads "BASE CASE   LIGHTS: 1 OF 1" from 75.33, not 0 OF 1. "1 … = F" at 76.00.
- 1998 3D basement: the camera pulls out of the beige monitor to the child at the desk, with a brightly lit wall. "0 … = F" at 80.00.

After that a light flickers on next to the child (83–84 s). Outside, the cut-away basement is lit and white puffs, which I took for smoke, rise from the chimney. The house windows light one by one from left to right (90.00–91.50 s) until all 7 are lit.

**9. The wall behind the child at the desk (80 s on)**
- It is made of pale grey-lavender bricks.
- A large, dark, jagged shadow shaped like a stepped triangle rises from behind the child almost to the top of the frame. It looks like a pine tree.
- A thin red/orange line runs along the top, and the wall seems to meet another wall at a corner on the right.
- In the foreground on the left are a tall cylinder with an orange band and a thin orange pipe. The dark back of the monitor is on the right.
- After the flicker (from 84.00 s) the shadow turns a much paler grey and the bricks look brighter.

**10. The last thing shown, and the words**
- The outside of the house at night, with snow falling and all 7 windows lit, and the cut-away basement lit underneath.
- Large white pixel letters read "BASE CASE" (from 92.50 s), with smaller text below: "EVERY HOUSE HAS ONE." (from 93.00 s). This holds until 95.83 s.

**11. What was unclear, too fast, or too small**
- **Too small:** the house picture and text on the beige monitor at 26–27 s, until the camera pushed in. The STACK box text was small throughout, but I could read it.
- **Too small or scrambled:** the text in the nested screens during the pull-back at about 76–78 s.
- **Garbled:** the caption during the grainy dissolve at 43.17–43.33 s.
- **Unclear:**
  - What happens at the trapdoor in 3D (11–12.5 s), and whether the child crouches there or at 34.5 s.
  - The brief white and orange flare on the basement cylinder (21.5–22.17 s).
  - The small grey object on the basement floor at about 22 s.
  - Whether the witch has one eye or two.
  - Who types the commands and who moves the green pointer.
  - What "F" means.
  - What casts the large shadow.
  - Why the lights went out at 5.00 s.
  - Why 7 windows are lit at the end when only 5 were at the start.
- **Too fast:** the flicker frames at 83.00–83.67 s and the single-frame inverted flashes in the Mac section (41.00, 43.00, 45.00 s).
