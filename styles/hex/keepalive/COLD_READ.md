# Keepalive: a cold reading

One reader, who had seen nothing and was told nothing, was given the film on 28 contact sheets: every sixth
picture (four a second; a blink of a lamp lasts six frames, so each is caught once). No questions were sent
afterwards.

Sealed beforehand (entry 10 of `../journal.md`): beats 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 would land; 4, 5 and 7 would be half,
because a stranger would count the blinks without being sure why three was refused and six accepted.

## The score

| | beat | result | in the reader's words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | an island at dusk, through someone's eyes; a hand clicks its way up | landed | "This side: never seen. It appears only as the hand cursor" |
| 2 | a book gives three rules | landed | quoted exactly, all three |
| 3 | the beacon is lit; the far tower blinks three times | landed | "a light flashes at the far tower and throws an orange streak across the water. The bottom line counts '< 1', '< 2', '< 3'" |
| 4 | they answer three and are refused | landed | "After one wrong answer (copying the three flashes back instead of answering four)" |
| 5 | sent five, they answer six; the far window stays lit | landed | "This time the answer is one more than what was received." |
| 6 | through the spyglass, a green witch; they wave; she does not | landed | "The figure does not move in any frame." "the reply is just '<' with nothing after it" |
| 7 | her lamp dips; the beacon dips back | landed | "Every four seconds the right light goes out briefly ... Half a second later the left light goes out" |
| 8 | nothing is sent; the lights only keep answering | landed | "the two lights settle into blinking 'STILL HERE.' at each other with nothing else to say" |

Eight of eight. I had predicted five and three halves, so I was wrong about three beats again, this time in
the other direction. What did it, by the reader's own account, was the line of system type along the bottom:
"From here on I read '>' as what this side sends and '<' as what it receives." A counted log made a puzzle
legible to somebody who was not allowed to play it.

## The narration, unedited

**What I saw, in order**

A small strip of coloured squares (blue, green, white, yellow, orange, red) sits in the bottom-right corner for the whole film. I don't know what it is. It might be a palette or some kind of meter. Every scene change is a dissolve through pixel noise, and almost every one comes just after the cursor moves to the bottom edge of the picture.

**0.00–3.75.** It is night, with a purple sky and a few stars. A grey speckled hill rises out of a dark, rippled sea, with pale pink-orange patches on its slope. On top of the hill is a small grey tower with a dark cap. There is a round orange thing to its left and a thin pink stick to its right. A shelter with a slatted roof stands on posts on the left shoulder of the hill. Far off to the right, out in the sea, is a tall dark tower with a pointed top on a wide base. I take it to be a lighthouse. Nothing in the scene moves. At 1.00 a white pointing-hand cursor appears and drifts up toward the shelter.

**4.00.** Closer to the shelter. The ground in front of it is now bright orange speckle.

**7.00.** Under the shelter is a grey table with a white sheet and a dark rectangle on it, and a grey cube in front of it like a stool. The cursor hovers over the sheet.

**10.00–14.75.** An open book fills the screen. The left page reads: "LOG" / "1. I ANSWER WHAT IS WELL FORMED." / "2. ANSWER ONE MORE THAN YOU ARE SENT." / "3. I DO NOT WAVE." Faint text at the foot of the page reads "THE LAMP. THE DIAL. THE GLASS." The right page has a box of grey static and the words "NO LINK." The cursor runs down the numbered lines.

**15.00.** A close view of the hilltop:
- The orange thing is a cog-shaped dial showing a zero with a slash through it.
- The tower is brick, with a dark lamp-room at the top.
- The pink stick is a lever with a white knob, on a grey post.

At 17.00 the lever is down, the lamp burns yellow, and a line of text appears along the bottom edge: "> HELLO".

**18.00.** A new view: the sea, the far tower, and in the foreground a grey tripod holding an orange tube. I guess this is a telescope, "the glass." At 20.00, 20.50 and 21.00 a light flashes at the far tower and throws an orange streak across the water. The bottom line counts "< 1", "< 2", "< 3". From here on I read ">" as what this side sends and "<" as what it receives.

**23.00–28.75.** Back at the lamp. The dial is clicked from 0 to 1, 2, 3 (24.50–25.50). The lever is flipped up and down again (27.00–27.25), and the lamp goes out and comes back three times while the bottom line shows "> 1", "> 2", "> 3".

**29.00–32.75.** The telescope view. At 31.00 the line reads "< NO." At the same moment the tube changes: its body turns dull grey and only its end stays orange. I can't tell whether it turned, dimmed, or something else happened.

**33.00–35.75.** The log again. The cursor stops on rule 2.

**36.00–37.75.** The lamp is off and the dial is at 3. The lever is pulled, the lamp comes on, and the line reads "> HELLO".

**38.00–41.75.** The far light flashes five times (39.00–41.00), counted "< 1" to "< 5".

**42.00–48.75.** The dial is clicked from 3 to 4, 5, 6. The lever is flipped and the lamp blinks six times, counted "> 1" to "> 6". This time the answer is one more than what was received.

**49.00–52.75.** At 50.00 the far light comes on and stays on, with a steady streak on the water. The line reads "< YES.  LINK UP." The tube is fully orange again. The cursor moves to the telescope.

**53.00–63.75.** A black screen with a circle in it, which I take to be the view through the telescope. Inside the circle is a grey brick arched window with a figure in it:
- It has a green face, a black pointed wide-brimmed hat and a dark purple robe, with a small green hand at the left.
- Beside it stands an upright yellow rectangle. I guess it is a lamp or a candle.
- Near the top of the arch is a dark ragged blot with white specks. It could be a broken pane, a cobweb or a bat.
- The figure does not move in any frame.

At 58.00, with the cursor on the figure, the line reads "> (YOU WAVE.)". At 60.00 the reply is just "<" with nothing after it. At 62.00 the yellow rectangle and the window go dark for one frame, and the line reads "< STILL HERE." At 63.00 it reads "> STILL HERE."

**64.00–69.75.** The log again. The static is gone. The right page now shows a yellow arch split down the middle on dark blue, which looks like the lit window with no figure in it. Below it: "LINK UP." / "0 SENT." / "0 RECEIVED." / "STILL HERE." The bottom line changes to "0 SENT.  0 RECEIVED."

**70.00–83.75.** A wide view of the sea with two lights far apart. On the left is a pale glow with a halo and no building visible, above a low mound. On the right is the far tower, lit. Both throw streaks on the water and stay on.

Every four seconds the right light goes out briefly (72.00, 76.00, 80.00) as "< STILL HERE." appears. Half a second later the left light goes out as "> STILL HERE." appears. By the earlier pattern, that makes the left glow this side's lamp seen from a distance.

At 76.00 a black band appears across the top with large yellow letters: "KEEPALIVE". At 80.00 a line appears under it: "NOTHING TO SEND.  STILL HERE." The film ends at 83.75 with this still going and no fade-out.

**Who is in it**

- **This side:** never seen. It appears only as the hand cursor, working the lamp, dial and telescope at the hilltop station. I'd guess a lone keeper or operator.
- **The far tower:** the other end of the signal link.
- **The figure in the far window:** it looks like a stock witch (green skin, pointed hat), but it never moves. It could be a person, a doll or a machine. The rules written as "I", the blank answer to the wave, and the stillness make me suspect a rule-bound keeper or an automaton. That is a guess.

**The story in three sentences**

A lone operator at a hilltop light finds a log of rules and no link to the tower across the water. After one wrong answer (copying the three flashes back instead of answering four), they follow the rule, answer five flashes with six, and the link comes up. Through the telescope they see a still figure in the far window that does not wave back, and the two lights settle into blinking "STILL HERE." at each other with nothing else to say.

**The three moments I understood least**

1. **31.00:** the telescope tube turns grey with only its end orange, at the moment "NO." arrives.
2. **64.00:** the log says "0 SENT." and "0 RECEIVED." after all the flashing in both directions.
3. **60.00–62.00:** the reply to the wave is a bare "<" with nothing after it, and the window goes dark for one frame. Together with the figure that never moves and the blot at the top of the arch, I can't say what the other side is.
