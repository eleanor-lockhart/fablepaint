# Persistence, first cut: a cold reading, and what it changed

One reader, who had seen nothing and was told nothing, was given the first cut on 29 contact sheets: every
fifth picture (4.8 a second; five does not divide twelve, so the numbers over the horses are not aliased by
the sampling), and one extra sheet of twelve consecutive pictures from 36.5 s at the film's true rate. No
questions were sent afterwards: the narration had already answered them.

My own verdict and prediction were written down first (entry 8 of `../journal.md`). The prediction was that six
of the eight beats in the brief would land (1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8) and two would be half (3 and 6).

## The score

| | beat | result | in the reader's words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a shed in snow at night; somebody goes in with a lamp | landed | "a lone keeper walks from a caravan into a dark shed". But: "I never saw the door open." |
| 2 | a carousel; switched on; it turns slowly | landed | "a switch box that it has just used"; TURNS "reaches 6.0 at 13.96, where it stays" |
| 3 | the horses are numbered, and each is carved in a different pose | **half** | "They have different leg positions"; that each *is* one pose of a stride was "My guess", made at the end, from the name Muybridge |
| 4 | a flashing lamp, lights out, much faster | landed | "the figure is pushing the ride past its rated speed" |
| 5 | it appears to stop, and the horses gallop on the spot | **half** | "The scene now looks frozen", but "I could not confirm from the stills that the twelve positions make one smooth stride. I suspect they do" |
| 6 | it has not stopped: a different horse in the same place in every picture | landed | "Each 1/24 s the next horse lands in exactly the same spot, so the carousel turns a full circle in half a second while looking still." |
| 7 | their shadows gallop behind them | **half** | "big black horse-shaped masses"; "They might be the lamp's shadows, or unlit horses of another ring." |
| 8 | slowed; wood again; he leaves; the film says it is itself still pictures | landed | "1728 is 72 s x 24, which is this film's length. So I read the closing line as being about the film's own still pictures" |

Five landed and three half. I predicted six and two, and was wrong about three of the eight: beat 6, which
I thought rested on four words in a window, landed completely, arithmetic and all, because of the numbers over
the horses; beat 5 was only half, because a stride cannot be seen in stills of twelve different horses unless
something shows the twelve side by side; and beat 7 was half. That is the second film running in which I rated
a shadow as working and a stranger could not say what it was.

## What the reader did not understand, and what was done

| the reader | the cause | the change (second cut) |
|---|---|---|
| "I could not confirm ... that the twelve positions make one smooth stride" | the poses are only ever seen one at a time, on twelve different horses | **the card.** From 15.0 a strip along the bottom is Muybridge's card, redrawn: each horse that passes the lamp is put on it in its place, until it reads ONE STRIDE, IN TWELVE HORSES. Locked, a mark runs along it at twenty-four a second: the horse in front of the lamp now |
| "big black horse-shaped masses ... might be the lamp's shadows, or unlit horses of another ring" | the shadowed drum was register 0: a hole, not a darker surface. (The same fault as the "pine tree" in *Base Case*.) | under the lamp the unlit parts get more ambient, so a shadow is the drum's own panel, one step up from the void, with its frame showing through |
| "I never saw the door open" | the first shot ended at a shut door and the second began inside | he reaches the door at 5.0 and slides it open; at the end he slides it shut |
| "The figure is not visible in this shot" (40 to 44); "mostly hidden behind the lever post" (60 to 66) | from that camera the keeper stood behind the lamp stand | the camera moved a metre and a half west; more spill from the lamp on him |
| "I cannot tell what the figure sees from what only the camera sees" | the lamp and the film both run at 24 | left. That is the gag: the film is the same machine, and the last card says so |
| the window lit at the end: "Either a light was left on inside, or something there is still running" | meant | left |

Also changed before the second cut, from my own looking: the horses' legs were too dark to read (a limb one
cube thick has no shape round it to face any way, so it was lit as if facing nowhere; fixed in the library,
with the lower legs now the coat's colour and the hooves gilt); the horse has a bigger head, an arched neck
and a deeper chest; the ride's own lights are brighter.

## The narration, unedited

I read all 29 sheets in order, then the extra sheet. I looked at nothing else. The film is silent and about 72 seconds long. It is blocky and pixel-styled, like a low-resolution 3D game. One small grey human figure is the only person I saw.

**What I saw, in order**

**0.00–5.83 s. Outside, at night.**
- It is snowing under a starry sky, on flat snowy ground.
- On the right is a large shed with plank walls. Its sign reads "GALLOPERS". Under the sign is a big double door that is dark blue and glows blue at its foot. A small window on the shed's right side is dark.
- On the left is a small blue box on dark legs or wheels, which I take to be a caravan. It has one lit yellow window, small steps at its right end and a short dark stub on the roof, possibly a stovepipe.
- At 0.00 a small grey figure is already in the pool of light beside the caravan steps. I did not see it come out. It walks steadily right toward the shed door. The camera drifts very slightly.
- A caption box appears at 1.04: "WINTER QUARTERS." At 2.50 two lines are added: "ONCE A YEAR THE RIDE IS RUN, FOR NOBODY, / TO SEE THAT IT STILL RUNS." The caption is gone at 5.62.
- At 5.83 the figure is right at the door. I never saw the door open.

**6.04–11.88 s. Inside the dark shed.**
- On the left is part of a carousel: white horses on gold poles under a red and pink canopy.
- The figure walks in from the right past a tall black rectangle, which I take to be the doorway seen from inside. A soft pool of light on the floor moves with it, so it is probably carrying a light.
- From 7.50 it stands still with one arm held out toward the carousel. There is a small yellow point at its hand and a short red horizontal line nearby.
- From 8.12 to 9.17, bright patches move across the horses and the carousel's panels. At 8.96 one white horse with a pink and blue saddle is lit brilliantly. Then the light fades. My guess is a hand torch sweeping over the ride. It could instead be the ride's own lights flickering.
- At 9.38 the figure is a dark silhouette beside a red rope strung between two short posts, with a small unreadable sign on a pole. A large pale lilac half-circle of light lies on the back wall. I take this to mean it has turned to shine the torch at the wall.
- From 9.58 to 10.83 it walks right, and the lilac light shrinks to a band near the right wall. It stops facing the wall at a box.
- From 11.04 the wall light fades, and a small red glow appears at its hand height on the box. I take this to be a switch box that it has just used.

**12.08–17.92 s. The hall lit, wide front view of the whole carousel.**
- The walls are lilac planks, with beams across the ceiling. On the left is a pink panel with white vertical bars; it might be an organ front, but nothing ever happens to it. In the right foreground is a grey box on a pole.
- A readout appears at the top right. It has a title bar "RIDE" and the line "TURNS 0.0 /MIN (RATED 6)". The font makes M look like H, but I read it as /MIN. Under the text is a row of small coloured squares, which never change meaning that I could see.
- The carousel starts to turn. TURNS rises 0.3, 0.9, 1.7, 2.6, 3.6, 4.4, 5.2, 5.7, and reaches 6.0 at 13.96, where it stays.
- The camera moves round a little. This reveals a dark post with a pink lever and an orange top, which I call "the lever post".
- From 15.00, small cyan number tags (01 to 12) float above the horses. At a fixed spot the numbers go up over time, so the front horses are moving left to right.

**18.12–23.96 s. Close-up at the edge of the platform, still at 6.0.**
- Horses pass slowly, about one every 0.8 s by the tags (09, 10, 11, 12, 01, 02, 03, 04).
- They have different leg positions, saddles in pink or blue, and orange and purple trim.

**24.17–28.33 s. From behind the figure, in the aisle between carousel and wall.**
- The figure is grey and blocky, with a dark purple top (cap or hair) and an orange patch at the collar.
- Also in the aisle: the grey box on a pole, the lever post, a bench with a pink seat, a small blue box, and brown stacked crates.
- At 24.58 three lines are added to the readout: "HORSES PASSING 1.2 /S", then "LAMP FLASHES 24.0 /S" and "NOT LOCKED", both dim grey.
- The figure walks to the lever post by 26.88.
- At 27.08 the whole hall goes dark. At 27.29 the box on the pole shows a yellow vertical bar and throws a cone of hard light. "LAMP FLASHES" turns bright; "NOT LOCKED" stays grey.
- I take the box to be a flashing lamp, and I think the figure switched the hall lights off and the lamp on.

**28.54–29.79 s. Close-up of the figure's grey hand on the pink lever, which has a white knob.**
- The TURNS text turns red, and the numbers climb: 6.7, 8.2, 10.2, 12.2, 14.1, 15.5. HORSES PASSING goes 1.3 to 3.1.
- The horses become a smeared, doubled, broken-up blur. I take this to mean the figure is pushing the ride past its rated speed.

**30.00–35.83 s. A wider shot, with the figure standing by the lever post under the lamp.**
- TURNS keeps climbing: 16.0, 23.3, 30.7 … 96.7, 104.0, then slowing as it nears the end: 118.1, 119.2, 119.8, 120.0 at 35.83. HORSES PASSING climbs to 24.0.
- The smear is worst in the middle of the climb. From about 33.5 s the picture clears, and near 24/s the horses look sharp again.
- At 35.83 the readout still says "NOT LOCKED".

**36.04–39.79 s. Close-up at the platform again.**
- The fourth line changes to "LOCKED: 1 HORSE A FLASH" in yellow, and stays that way to 59.79.
- The scene now looks frozen: the same layout, same posts, a horse in the same place. But the tags change every sampled frame: 03, 08, 01, 06, 11, 04, 09, 02, 07, 12, 05 …
- The horses are bright white with hard black shadows.
- **The extra sheet (36.500–36.958 s, consecutive frames) settles this.** The centre tag goes 02, 03, 04 … 12, 01, one step per picture, and the side tags step up too. Each 1/24 s the next horse lands in exactly the same spot, so the carousel turns a full circle in half a second while looking still.
- The centre horse's legs are in a different position in each picture. I could not confirm from the stills that the twelve positions make one smooth stride. I suspect they do, because of the closing text.

**40.00–43.96 s. Wide front view with the lever post in the right foreground.**
- Still locked. The horses hold their places while the tags cycle.
- The figure is not visible in this shot.

**44.17–55.83 s.**
- 44.17: a low wide view from another side. The tags are gone.
- 48.12: a platform close-up, with frozen white horses and big black horse-shaped masses among them.
- 52.08: over the figure's shoulder, its hand on the lever, looking at the frozen ride.

**56.04–59.79 s. From high above.**
- The centre drum is hollow at the top.
- The tags are back, and the numbers repeat, so I think there are two or three rings of twelve horses (a guess).
- The layout stays fixed while every tag changes each frame.
- A small grey shape is near the lever post. From 59.17 a figure seems to be lower right, apart from the lever. I can't tell whether it or the camera moved.

**60.00–65.83 s. Wide view, with the figure at the lever.**
- "NOT LOCKED" returns, and the ride slows: 119.9, 119.5 … 102.1, 92.1, 78.7, 63.4.
- The horses smear again.
- At 63.12 the hall lights come back on and "LAMP FLASHES" goes dim.
- TURNS falls: 47.5, 32.4, 19.6, 10.4. At 6.1 (63.96) the text turns white again. It continues 5.9 … 3.0, 2.1, 1.2, 0.6, and ends at 0.1 /MIN with 0.0 /S at 65.83.
- In the last shots a grey shape mostly hidden behind the lever post is probably the figure.

**66.04–71.88 s. Outside again, the same view.**
- The shed's right-hand window is now lit yellow; at the start it was dark. The blue glow at the foot of the door is gone.
- The figure walks from the door left toward the caravan.
- 69.17: a yellow title appears at the top, "PERSISTENCE". The shed's roof also looks darker from here on; this may be a camera change.
- 70.62: added in white, "1728 PICTURES. NONE OF THEM MOVED.", and below it in small grey letters, "THE HORSES ARE FROM MUYBRIDGE, 1878."
- At the last frame (71.88) the figure is near the caravan steps but has not reached them.

**What I take it to mean**
- The ride is rated at 6 turns a minute, and with 12 horses a ring that gives 1.2 horses a second.
- At 120 turns a minute exactly 24 horses pass each second. That matches the lamp's 24 flashes and the film's 24 pictures a second, so each flash, and each picture, catches the next horse in the same spot.
- 1728 is 72 s × 24, which is this film's length. So I read the closing line as being about the film's own still pictures, and perhaps also the fixed carved horses.
- From outside knowledge, not the screen: Muybridge photographed a galloping horse as a series of stills in 1878. My guess is that each carousel horse copies one of those stills, so that when locked they should look like one horse galloping on the spot.
- One limit: because the lamp and the film both run at 24, I cannot tell what the figure sees from what only the camera sees.

**Who or what is in it**
- **The figure:** one person. I can't tell sex or age. It wears grey, with a dark purple cap or hair and an orange collar or scarf. It is probably the caretaker and probably lives in the caravan.
- **The carousel:** "GALLOPERS". It has numbered white horses, 01 to 12 in each ring.
- **The flashing lamp:** the grey box on a pole.
- **The lever post:** the speed control.
- **The readout** and the floating number tags: overlays that no one in the film seems to see.
- **The caravan.**
- I saw no one else, which fits "FOR NOBODY".

**The story in three sentences**
In a snowbound winter quarters, a lone keeper walks from a caravan into a dark shed, finds a switch by torchlight, and starts a stored carousel at its rated six turns a minute. The keeper switches to a flashing lamp and pushes the ride to 120 turns a minute, twenty times its rating, until it reads "LOCKED: 1 HORSE A FLASH" and the spinning ride looks frozen, a new numbered horse in each place every 1/24 second. The keeper then slows it to a stop, the lights come back on, and the keeper walks back toward the caravan while the title "PERSISTENCE" and the note about 1728 pictures and Muybridge's horses appear.

**The three moments I understood least**
1. **The locked section, 36–60 s.** I can't tell whether the freezing, and any gallop in place, is something the keeper sees by the lamp or only an effect of the 24-a-second camera. I also don't know what the large black horse-shaped masses are. They might be the lamp's shadows, or unlit horses of another ring.
2. **Where the keeper is while the ride runs at twenty times its rating.** The keeper is not visible at 40–44 s and seems away from the lever at 59 s, then is back at it at 60 s.
3. **The ending outside.** The shed window is lit at the end but was dark at the start, and the door's blue glow is gone. Either a light was left on inside, or something there is still running. Close behind is the light sweep and lilac half-circle at 8–10 s, which I guess was a torch but could be the ride's own lights.
