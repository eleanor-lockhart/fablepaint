# Persistence, second cut: a second cold reading

A new reader (a reader works once), the whole second cut, on the same terms as the first: every fifth picture
on 29 sheets, and twelve consecutive pictures from 36.5 s. No questions were sent afterwards.

Sealed beforehand (entry 9 of `../journal.md`): beats 3 and 5 would land now, because of the card; beat 7
would land ("they will say shadows"); the door would be seen opening; the keeper would be found in every
shot. Eight of eight, or seven with beat 7 still half.

Seven landed and one was missed. The card did what it was for: "Read in order, the twelve silhouettes make one
gallop." The keeper was found in every shot ("the figure watching from the lower right"; "The figure stands by
the lever the whole time").

The shadows went the wrong way. In the first cut they were register 0 and a stranger saw "big black
horse-shaped masses" and did not know what they were. In the second they are the drum's own panels, darker,
which is right, and the second stranger did not see them at all. Both times I had predicted they would land.
What I now think is true: **a shadow among the things that cast it is texture.** For a shadow to be an event it
needs a clear surface, a shot of its own, and nothing else in the frame to look at; this ride has two rings of
horses standing in front of the only surface the shadows fall on. I have not found the shot. It stays missed.

Still not understood by either reader: what exactly happens in the dark between 7.5 and 12 s (both guessed a
torch and a switch, and both were right, but both were guessing); how many horses there are (two rings of
twelve; "I can't tell whether there are 12 horses or several dozen"); and the window lit at the end, which is
meant to be left open.

After this reading one shot was changed: the move round the ride at 44 s ("the tags vanish and the light turns
flatter and greyer") now keeps the numbers and goes round the lit side, away from the lamp, so that the shadows
come out from behind the horses as the camera leaves the lamp's line. It has not been read cold.

## The narration, unedited

**Narration of the film, in order**

**0.00–5.0 s, outside at night.** Snow falls and the sky is full of stars. A long shed made of vertical purple-grey planks fills the frame. A small sign above its big blue door reads "GALLOPERS". To the left is a small dark-blue box on legs, with steps and one lit yellow window. I take it to be a caravan, though it could be a hut. The shed has a small window on its right wall, and it is dark. A small grey figure walks from the caravan steps, across a patch of lit snow, to the door. The camera drifts slowly. At 1.04 s a caption appears: "WINTER QUARTERS." At 2.50 s it gains two lines: "ONCE A YEAR THE RIDE IS RUN, FOR NOBODY, / TO SEE THAT IT STILL RUNS."

**5.42–5.83 s.** The door slides aside and the figure stands in the lit gap. The caption disappears.

**6.04–7.29 s, inside and dark.** Part of a carousel sits on the left: white horses with blue and pink saddles on gold poles, a pink-red canopy and a wooden platform. The open doorway is a black slab on the right. The figure walks in and stops in a pale circle of light on the floor.

**7.50–9.17 s.** The figure holds one arm out toward the carousel. In the hand is something small with red and yellow points. From 8.33 to 8.96 s the horses brighten, then go dark again. My guess is a torch beam passing over them. It could also be the lights flickering.

**9.38–11.88 s.** A violet half-dome of light appears on the back wall to the right, with a small stand holding a red bar in front of it. I cannot tell what either one is. The figure walks to that spot, then to a box on the right wall, and reaches into it. Guess: he is switching on the power.

**12.08 s, cut to a brightly lit wide shot.** The whole carousel sits in the middle of the shed under roof beams. On the left is a pink-red panel with white vertical bars; it might be an organ front, or a chart. On the right in the foreground is a grey box on a post. A readout in the top right says "RIDE", then "TURNS 0.0 /MIN (RATED 6)", with a row of small coloured squares underneath. The pixel font draws M like H, so on screen it looks like "/HIN". The count rises steadily and reaches 6.0 at 13.96 s. As the camera slides, a short post with an orange top comes into view.

**15.00–23.96 s.** Cyan number tags (01–12) appear over the horses. A bar along the bottom reads "IN FRONT OF THE LAMP: HORSE 05" on the left and "1 OF 12 ON THE CARD" on the right. Below it is a strip of 12 cells, and cell 05 fills with the silhouette of a galloping horse. About every 0.83 s the next horse is named and its cell fills: 06 is "2 OF 12", and so on. At 18.12 s the camera cuts down among the passing horses. By 23.33 s the bar reads "11 OF 12".

**24.17 s.** We see the figure from behind, in the foreground. He is blocky, with a grey coat and a purple cap. By the wall stands a black square on a thin stand. All twelve cells are full, and the right-hand text changes to "ONE STRIDE, IN TWELVE HORSES." Read in order, the twelve silhouettes make one gallop. At 24.58 s the readout adds "HORSES PASSING 1.2 /S", then "LAMP FLASHES 24.0 /S" and "NOT LOCKED", both in grey. The figure walks to the orange-topped post.

**27.08 s.** Everything goes dark. **27.29 s.** The black square now shows a lit yellow slit, so it is the lamp. "LAMP FLASHES" turns bright.

**28.54–29.79 s, close-up.** The figure, with orange at the neck or face, pushes a pink lever with a white knob on an orange base. "TURNS" turns red and the number climbs: 6.7, 8.2, 10.2 … 15.5.

**30.00–35.83 s.** We are back at the side view, with the figure standing by the lever. TURNS climbs through 60.0 at 31.25 s to 120.0 at 35.83 s, and HORSES PASSING climbs toward 24.0. Around 31–33 s the carousel smears into ghosted streaks. It sharpens as the numbers close in on 24. Meanwhile the horse named "in front of the lamp" jumps around irregularly.

**36.04 s.** "LOCKED: 1 HORSE A FLASH" appears in yellow, and we are close among the horses. In my five-a-second sample the horse in front of the lamp jumps five numbers at a time (03, 08, 01, 06…). The extra sheet of consecutive frames (36.500–36.958 s) shows what really happens. On each frame the next horse (02, 03 … 12, 01) sits in exactly the same spot, and the background horses do too; only the legs change. At true speed, then, the carousel would look frozen while its horses gallop in place, one stride every half second. I take this to be the point of the film.

**40.00–43.96 s.** A wider shot, still locked, with the figure watching from the lower right. **44.17 s.** The tags vanish and the light turns flatter and greyer while the camera moves. At 48.12 s we are close again. At 52.08 s we look over the figure's shoulder: purple cap and orange collar, with the lever's white knob in the foreground.

**56.04–59.79 s, overhead.** We look down on the carousel with the canopy gone. A central drum is ringed by horses several rows deep, with tags 01–12 repeating, and the figure is tiny at the right. Guess: there are more than twelve horses, arranged in sets of twelve.

**60.00 s.** Back to the wide shot. "NOT LOCKED" returns while TURNS still reads 120.0. Then it falls: 102.1 at 62.29 s, with the streaks back. At 63.12 s "LAMP FLASHES" greys out and the room light returns. At 63.96 s TURNS reads 6.1 and is white again. It drops to 0.1 /MIN, with "HORSES PASSING 0.0 /S", at 65.83 s. The figure stands by the lever the whole time.

**66.04 s, outside.** The door is open and the figure stands in the gap. By 66.67 s the door is shut and he is in front of it, so I infer he closed it. He walks left toward the caravan. One thing is new: the shed's right-hand window is now lit yellow. At 69.17 s the title "PERSISTENCE" appears. At 70.62 s two lines are added: "1728 PICTURES. NONE OF THEM MOVED." and, in small type, "THE HORSES ARE FROM MUYBRIDGE, 1878." The film ends at 71.88 s with the figure near the caravan steps. My own arithmetic: 1728 is 72 s × 24 frames, so the line probably describes this film as well as the horses.

**Who is in it**
- **The figure:** one small, blocky person in a grey coat and purple cap, apparently living in the caravan. Most likely the ride's keeper during the winter.
- **The carousel (the "GALLOPERS"):** each horse is a still model in a different moment of one stride. That matches the twelve silhouettes on the "card", which the end text credits to Muybridge, 1878.
- **The lamp:** a black box on a stand that flashes 24 times a second.
- **The readout:** an unseen instrument or narrator that names, counts and judges.

**The story in three sentences**
On a snowy winter night, a lone keeper walks from his caravan into a shed holding a stored carousel, for its once-a-year test run with nobody watching. He starts it at its rated speed, switches on a flashing lamp, and drives the ride to twenty times that speed until exactly one horse passes the lamp per flash. At that point the carousel seems to stand still while its twelve frozen horses gallop as one, and then he slows it to a stop, leaves, and the film names itself "PERSISTENCE".

**The three moments I understood least**
1. **7.5–11.9 s:** the outstretched arm, the horses brightening, the violet half-dome with the red-barred stand, and the reach into the wall box. I am only guessing at torch, lights and power switch.
2. **44.17–59.79 s:** the tags vanish and the light changes, and then the overhead view shows numbers repeating in rings. I can't tell whether there are 12 horses or several dozen, or why the view changed.
3. **66–71.9 s:** the shed window is lit at the end when it was dark at the start. Something was left on, or someone else is inside; I can't tell which.
