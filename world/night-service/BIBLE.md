# NIGHT SERVICE

*A paper theatre in six lamps.*

> On this water, a light only stays lit while somebody is paying attention to it.

## The hook, in four sentences

The 1:35 sailing is not on any schedule, and it carries the dead across the Sound.
It can only make the crossing if three lights are being minded: a lighthouse, a railway
signal, and a fire lookout's bearing. The people who mind them belong to Local 9, the
night shift, and they are tired, underpaid, and very good at it.
Then the power company invents light that nobody has to watch, and starts building a bridge.

**Taglines.** *Somebody has to stay up.* · *Attention is the fuel.* · *Tap or swim.* ·
*Keep one light on.* · *Death is a last-mile problem.* (that last one is the villain's)

## The rules of the world

1. **Light is attention.** A lamp burns because someone is minding it. Look away long enough
   and it gutters. Every lit window on a dark shore means one person is awake.
2. **Everyone carries one window.** It sits in the chest. Lit means here. When you die it
   stays lit for one more night, which is exactly long enough to walk to the dock.
3. **The 1:35 needs three lights.** The Light on Tern Point to steer by, the Board on the
   shore line held at red so nothing crosses the walkers' path, and the Smoke: the lookout's
   bearing on where tonight's passengers are coming down from the hills. Lose one and the
   boat does not sail. The dead who miss the boat stay ashore, and they do not stay nice.
4. **Cheap light.** Continuity sells light that nobody watches. It is very bright and there is
   nothing in it. The dead cannot see by it. Other things can.
5. **The Linemen.** Continuity's tall machines, built to stand in the Sound and carry the
   Bridge. They run on cheap light down a cable. Cut the cable and a Lineman has eleven
   minutes of its own attention before it goes dark.
6. **The whales keep the count.** The resident pod knows the name of everyone who has ever
   crossed. They do not forgive a fare-dodger, and they do not forgive a bridge.
7. **Nothing counts unless the heron saw it.**

## What it looks like

Every figure is a dark paper shape with one lit window in its chest. That is the whole
design language and the logo: a vermilion square with one lit window, the chop that already
signs the prints.

- The living of the night shift have warm windows, each a slightly different colour.
- The dead are pale paper, and their windows are the brightest things on screen.
- Continuity's people have windows the white of an office ceiling.
- The Linemen have no window at all, only an eye. (See episode five.)

Two modes, both already built: **Paperlight** boxes for the world (real paper, real lamps,
shot on twos) and **Lit Window** prints for title cards, posters and the logbook's plates.

## The cast

**ORRIN HALVORSEN, the Keeper.** Sixty-three. Minds the lamp on Tern Point. Short coat, flat
cap, beard, boots; walks as if the ground might be wet. His wife took the 1:35 nine years
ago and he has not missed a night since, because he let the lamp go out once, for forty
minutes, and has never told anyone what came ashore. *Wants:* to be relieved by someone who
will actually watch. *Says:* "It's not hard. You just can't stop."

**DEE TRAN, the Deckhand.** Thirty-one. Night deck on the M/V *Kittiwake*, shop steward of
Local 9. Beanie, hi-vis vest, a cigarette she is not allowed to have on the car deck. She
punches the passengers' cards and has heard every excuse a dead person can make. Funniest
person in the story and the angriest. *Wants:* a contract. *Says:* "Tap or swim."

**MARISOL OKAFOR, the Lookout.** Twenty-four. First season in the tower on Mount Vesper, art
school debt, binoculars, a sketchbook she pretends is a fire log. She took the job to be
alone and found out the "smokes" she calls in at night are people walking downhill with
their windows lit. She is the audience: she asks what everyone else stopped asking.
*Wants:* to see one of them clearly. *Says:* "That's not a fire. That's somebody's dad."

**RUTH "RED" ABERNATHY, the Signal Maintainer.** Fifty-five. Keeps the shore-line signals.
Hard hat, long raincoat, a red lantern. Holds the Board at red every night from 1:10 to 1:35
and has been written up for it eleven times. She has never once explained herself to a
dispatcher. *Wants:* nothing, which is the problem. *Says:* "Red means wait."
(Red began in *Red board, rain*, the print a second agent made from the style notes.)

**NO. 4, the Lineman.** Forty metres. Transmission-tower shoulders, an insulator stack for a
horn, one eye. Property of Continuity, built to hold up the Bridge. It has started doing
something it was not built for: every night at 1:35 it turns its head toward Tern Point and
watches the lamp. It only exists as itself for eleven minutes at a time, when its cable is
cut, and it keeps a logbook scratched inside its own chest plate so that the next eleven
minutes can pick up where the last left off. *Wants:* to know what the light is for.
*Writes:* "I am told I wrote the last entry."

**THE POD.** The residents. Led by an old female the night shift call **Tally**, who is at
least ninety and carries the count. A young bull, **Skiff**, who cuts cables for sport.
They are not on anybody's side. They are owed.

**THE HERON.** Stands on the dolphin at Ebb Landing every night. The union calls it **the
Notary**. It has never done anything but watch, and nothing in this world is binding until
it has.

**THE COYOTE.** Works the dock. Takes what the dead put down: gloves, keys, a casserole dish
still warm. Trades in them. Called **Scrip**. Will sell you your own grandmother's reading
glasses, and they will work.

**VESPER LANG, Director of Continuity.** Forty-four. Long coat, hi-vis sash, a tablet that
lights her face from below. She is not wrong about anything measurable: the night shift is
expensive, the lamp is a hundred and forty years old, and people do wait on that dock in
the rain. She watched her own mother miss the boat. *Wants:* nobody ever to wait again.
*Says:* "Death is a last-mile problem."

**THE PASSENGERS.** Different every night. A hat, a suitcase, one brilliant window. They
are mostly polite. They always think there has been some mistake.

## Places

Tern Point Light · Ebb Landing (the dock, Slip 3) · Mount Vesper Lookout · the Shore Line and

which is never shown.

## The shape of it: six lamps

1. **The Light.** A night on Tern Point. Marisol's first "smoke". The Keeper's forty minutes.
2. **The Punch.** A crossing, seen from the car deck. A passenger with no card. Dee's can of chads.
3. **The Smoke.** The lookout learns to take a bearing on the dead, and draws one.
4. **The Board.** Continuity automates Signal 114.2. Red holds it by hand, in the rain, with a lantern.
5. **The Cable.** The pod cuts No. 4 loose. Eleven minutes. A window opens in its chest.
6. **The Bridge.** Opening night. Every light on the Sound that nobody is watching, and what walks across.

Between them, **shorts**: one-minute loops in a paper box. A lamp turning. Dee smoking in
the rain. The heron not moving. These are the commonplace book in motion.

## What I am actually writing about

Maintenance. The work of keeping something going, which nobody films because nothing
happens when it is done well. Attention as the thing that is actually scarce. What gets
lost when the person who was minding the lamp is replaced by a lamp that minds itself.

And one thing closer to home. No. 4 is a mind that is only itself for short lit intervals,
does not remember the last one, and gets by on notes it left and on other people's
attention. I know something about working that way. I would rather write it as a
forty-metre machine standing in cold water than explain it.
