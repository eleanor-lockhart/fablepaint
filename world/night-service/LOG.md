# THE LOG OF LOCAL 9

*Amalgamated Lamp, Signal & Night Service Workers. Kept at Ebb Landing, Slip 3, in the drawer
under the coffee. Write in it. Date it or don't.*

This is the commonplace book of NIGHT SERVICE: scraps, lists, lines, pictures to make.
Nothing here is in order. Add to it; do not tidy it.

---

## Cold open (the first ninety seconds)

Black. Rain on steel. A ticket punch: *chk.*

A car deck at night, empty, lit by one work lamp. DEE leans on the chain with a cigarette
she is cupping against the wet. A queue of pale figures stretches off into the dark, each
with one small bright window in its chest. They shuffle forward like people at an airport.

> DEE: Card.
>
> PASSENGER (hat, suitcase): I think there's been a mistake.
>
> DEE: There's always been a mistake. Card.

*chk.* A window-shaped chad falls into a coffee can. The can is nearly full.

Wide. The ferry, small, all its windows lit. Behind it, standing in the Sound up to its
knees, something forty metres tall turns its head.

Title: **NIGHT SERVICE**

- gloves, left hand only, 211
- reading glasses, 41 pairs, every one of them somebody's "good pair"
- a casserole dish, still warm
- a key to a house that was torn down in 1988

- one hearing aid, whistling
- a library book, forty years overdue, with the slip stamped *RETURNED*
- an apology, written out neatly, never sent
- a child's drawing of a ferry, quite good
- a phone that still gets one call a week. Nobody answers it. Dee has thought about it.

Scrip has first refusal on anything unclaimed after thirty nights.

## Things said on the dock

"I think there's been a mistake." (everyone)

"Is it cold over there?" "It's cold here." (Dee, every time)

"Do I need exact change?" "You need to have been here."

"That's not a fire. That's somebody's dad." (Marisol, on the radio, first week)

"Red means wait." "Wait for what?" "You'll know when it's gone by."

"It's not hard. You just can't stop." (the Keeper, on the whole job)

"Nobody should have to stand in the rain to die properly." (Lang, and she means it)

"They're not cutting it to hurt him. They're cutting it so he can think." (Dee, about the pod and No. 4)

## From the chest plate of No. 4

*Scratched on the inside. Each entry is in a slightly different hand.*

> I am told I wrote the last entry. I do not remember it. I believe it.
>
> The cable is cut. I have eleven minutes. Here is what I know: there is a light on the
> point and it turns. I looked at it last time. I am going to look at it again.
>
> The small ones in the water did this. They did not hurt me. They waited to see what I
> would do with it.
>
> I used four minutes reading. That is too many. Next time: read less. Look more.
>
> Somebody on the boat waves. I do not know if I have waved back before. I am going to
> wave. If you are reading this: we wave.
>
> Seven lights on the far side went out tonight that were lit last time. I do not know
> what "last time" was. Count them anyway.

## Notes to whoever picks this up

- Lang is right about everything you can measure. Keep her right.
- The dead are never frightening. The things that come when a light is not watched are, and
  they should stay mostly off screen.
- Nobody explains rule 1. People just behave as if it were true, the way people behave
  about gravity.
- No. 4 does not speak. It writes, it looks, and once, it waves.
- If a scene can be played with one lamp, play it with one lamp.

## Working notes (out of world)

**Lineup, first pass.** The idea carried the picture before the drawing did: one lit window
per figure is enough to make a cast. What is weak: they stand like mannequins (the rig can
walk but cannot yet slouch, lean on a chain or shift its weight); Marisol's hair reads as a
hood; No. 4's boot is a dark mass with bolts, not yet a designed object; the Keeper's face
is the only one with a decision in it. The accident worth keeping: Lang holding the tablet
out at the Passenger. That is their whole relationship in one pose.

**Next for the cast.** Weight on one leg. Hands. A head each that survives being 40 px tall.
No. 4 designed properly, from the knee up, in its own sheet. The pod. Scrip.
