# Roadmap

What is queued, in the order Ellie asked for it. Tick things off here; the detail lives in each folder's journal.

1. **The opening sequence** (`world/night-service/op/`): done as a prototype.
   - [x] storyboard, reviewed by three independent agents, revised
   - [x] ten units and the theme; assembled; sync measured; published (v1, tag `op-v1`)
   - [x] detailed critique by an independent Opus critic (`CRITIQUE.md`, 5/10)
   - [x] v1.1 from the critique, tested on a cold reader (`COLD_READ_v1.1.md`); retrospective (`RETROSPECTIVE.md`)
   - [ ] **version 2**, after studies 2 to 4 below, as their test. Known and waiting: Orrin's doze still does not
     read (stage him lit, facing us, eyes closing); the dead and Continuity are both "white" (give the dead a
     light of their own); 24 frames a second with the camera on ones; bodies that differ, hands, follow-through;
     No. 4's insulator horn and lattice shoulders; one model per shared prop; a verse tune and a chorus that
     peaks at its end; break the two-second grid; nobody has yet heard the theme (questions in `music/JOURNAL.md`).
2. **Spatial understanding.** Think out a physical environment first; model it as vector / CAD drawings (plan,
   elevation, section) and as a 3D space; then derive perspective and camera motion from the model for pictures,
   animation and the world's places (Tern Point, Ebb Landing, Mount Vesper, the *Kittiwake*). The opening's
   review gives this its first questions: where is the moon, how big is No. 4 next to a person, and what does a
   camera see when it moves through a set on ones.
3. **Bone-based animation.** Research how rigs are actually built and driven (hierarchies, FK/IK, constraints,
   skinning and deformation, easing and curves, secondary motion) and extend `backlot.figure` beyond rigid
   paper parts.
4. **Colour, fill, texture and detail for animated figures.** How to paint a figure that moves: flats and
   shadow shapes, line, texture that stays put on the body, costume detail, faces.

Standing rules: every asset is made in code; references are for looking and measuring; OpenArt only as
reference, never in a finished piece; the Muybridge plates stay out of the public repository.
